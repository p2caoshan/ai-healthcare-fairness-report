Cohorts and definitions

Each row is a paired SaO2 (arterial blood gas) and SpO2 (pulse oximeter), both restricted to 70–100. On MIMIC-IV v3.1 the pair is built in Spark: lab item 50817 with ICU chart item 220277, same patient and calendar date, within five minutes, and inside the admission window. That join yields 32,751 pairs. The modeling table has 30,454 rows and 44 columns; the notebooks do not print why those two counts differ. Labs are the nearest prior result within 24 hours. ICU vitals are the nearest prior chart value within one hour. BOLD is the released PhysioNet blood-gas table: 49,093 rows and 142 columns.

Skin groups, and who is in the test set

MIMIC maps free-text race into Light (White, European, Portuguese), Medium (Hispanic, Latino, Asian, Pacific, Indian, South American), Dark (Black, African), and Other/Unknown. Cutoffs in the code. Bias is SaO2 minus SpO2. Negative means the pulse oximeter reads higher than the artery. Hypoxemia is SaO2 under 88. The device “detects” it only if SpO2 is also under 88. A false negative is arterial hypoxemia with SpO2 still at 88 or above. A separate disparate-impact check treats SpO2 at or above 92 as the favorable “safe” reading. That is not the same rule as the research definition SaO2 < 88 and SpO2 ≥ 92, which these notebooks do not compute.

What the two cohorts support

Shared. Dark-group SpO2 over-reads relative to Light. A parity check at 92 still passes, and still sits above the arterial safe rate. Linear regression is a poor hypoxemia alarm. XGBoost, when it is the section 2 model, raises Dark-group detection sharply. pO2 dominates the fit. Group-loss search does not make the alarm fairer.
Not shared. MIMIC’s device error and hypoxemia rate are much higher. Dark versus Medium is significant only on BOLD. Medium is the weak detection group on MIMIC only. BOLD’s strong detector and BOLD’s Fairlearn refit are different trainings, and only one of them works.

The five metrics agree: the forest leads, age alone does not. Brier score here is the 0/1 error rate, not a probability score. Calibration plots tell a different story. Both logistic models track the diagonal. The forest and the booster are over-confident, because they were trained to classify rather than to match probabilities. If the number shown to a patient is a risk, calibration outranks the table. Net benefit was plotted, not tabulated: the three full-feature models beat treat-all and treat-none across a wide band of thresholds, roughly 0.1 to 0.9, and their expected cost falls inside that reference triangle. On that decision view the trees outrank logistic regression.

Appendix. Salk vaccine field trial

Design only. No records.

Handing the 1954 polio vaccine to a large group of children and counting cases the next year would not show that the vaccine worked. There is no control series, and nothing separates the vaccine from the other reasons polio rates move. Consistency, positivity, and ignorability are all missing. It is also the wrong first exposure for children when the evidence is still from the laboratory.

Vaccinating only the children whose parents consent is not randomization. Consent tracks exposure, schooling, and trust in medicine, and those same factors change polio risk. Ignorability fails.

Ask permission to be in the study. Then randomize the children who are in it.

Limits

Race is not melanin, and the two maps are not the same map. One split, no intervals on the fairness metrics, and thin event counts for Medium and for BOLD’s smaller groups. Median fill invents a typical lab for patients who were never measured. The fairness penalty optimizes a regression loss and is scored as a classifier. On BOLD, the model that detects and the model that is constrained are not the same training. Nothing on this page is a reason, by itself, to change a monitor or a treatment threshold.
