Appendix. Salk vaccine field trial

Design only. No records.

Handing the 1954 polio vaccine to a large group of children and counting cases the next year would not show that the vaccine worked. There is no control series, and nothing separates the vaccine from the other reasons polio rates move. Consistency, positivity, and ignorability are all missing. It is also the wrong first exposure for children when the evidence is still from the laboratory.

Vaccinating only the children whose parents consent is not randomization. Consent tracks exposure, schooling, and trust in medicine, and those same factors change polio risk. Ignorability fails.

Ask permission to be in the study. Then randomize the children who are in it.

Limits

Race is a proxy for melanin, and the two maps differ. One split, no intervals on the fairness metrics, and thin event counts for Medium and for BOLD’s smaller groups. Median fill invents a typical lab for patients who were never measured. The fairness penalty optimizes a regression loss and is scored as a classifier. On BOLD, the model that detects and the model that is constrained are different trainings. The section 2 recall spreads are arithmetic on rounded rates. Group calibration was plotted and not printed as a slope. Nothing on this page is a reason, by itself, to change a monitor or a treatment threshold.

Standalone methods note

Causal analysis in healthcare

How treatment effects are identified from trials, claims, and electronic records. This note does not use the pulse-oximetry cohorts, the heart-disease table, or any patient file.

The estimators in the code tab draw their own rows in memory. Nothing below is a result from MIMIC, BOLD, or a hospital extract.

What a healthcare analysis is usually asked to estimate

A causal question names a treatment strategy, the people it would cover, a start of follow-up, and an outcome. The number people want is a contrast between two strategies on that same group: the outcome if everyone followed one strategy, minus the outcome if everyone followed the other. An association between “was treated” and “later had the event” is a different number. Treated patients are often sicker, older, or already on a path that changes the outcome.

Routine practice data can still support that contrast when the design copies a trial that could have been run. The methods below are the ones regulators, health-technology groups, and clinical researchers use for that job. They differ in which door they close, and in which door they leave open.

A trial is the reference design

Random assignment makes the treated and untreated groups exchangeable at the start. Consistency says the outcome under the assigned strategy is the outcome we record. Positivity says every eligible person could have received either strategy. Ignorability is the claim that assignment does not track prognosis. A randomized trial is built so that third claim is plausible.

The 1954 polio field trial, summarized in the fairness tab, is the clean version of this design: permission first, then randomization among the children who are in the study. Consent-only assignment does not supply ignorability. The same three conditions reappear, under other names, in every method below.

Target trial emulation

When a trial is absent, the working practice is to write the trial protocol anyway and then emulate it in claims or an electronic record. Miguel Hernán and James Robins set out that two-step form: specify eligibility, treatment strategies, assignment, follow-up, the outcome, and the causal contrast; then line each piece up with the observational data. Time zero is the moment a person meets eligibility and a strategy is assigned. Starting the clock later, or letting post-treatment information into the eligibility rule, builds in immortal time and selection.

The FDA Sentinel Innovation Center’s PRINCIPLED guide (Rothman and colleagues, 2024) walks through the same sequence for drug effects in routine-care data: a protocol, an explicit emulation, confounders tied to treatment choice and to risk, and an estimator matched to whether treatment is a one-time start or a strategy that continues. Subgroup contrasts are effect-modification questions, specified in advance. A multi-database safety study of infliximab and non-infectious uveitis used the same frame, with the protocol and the analysis code posted separately from the patient tables.

One decision at baseline: propensity scores

If the strategy is chosen once, at baseline, measured confounding can be handled by a model for who gets treated. The propensity score is the probability of treatment given the measured covariates (Rosenbaum and Rubin, 1983). Healthcare studies use it in four ways.

UseWhat is comparedWhat is discarded or inflated
MatchingTreated people and untreated people with a similar scorePeople who find no pair. The remaining group is whoever could be matched, which is often closer to the treated population than to everyone eligible.
StratificationOutcomes inside bands of the scoreLittle. Residual imbalance can remain inside a wide band.
Inverse-probability weightingA reweighted sample that resembles the whole eligible group, or the treated group, depending on the weightNobody is dropped. A person with a probability near 0 or 1 receives a huge weight.
Overlap weightingThe people for whom either strategy was plausibleThe tails, on purpose. The target population is defined by the overlap, not chosen in advance.

A Clinical Infectious Diseases commentary on COVID-19 therapeutics makes the population point explicit: matching and overlap weights do not automatically estimate the effect in a population you named beforehand. Positivity is the practical check. If one group has no one with a given history, that history cannot be balanced. Balance tables after weighting, and the distribution of weights, are part of the result. A large-scale propensity model, as in the uveitis safety study, puts diagnoses, drugs, procedures, and labs into a regularized logistic regression and then matches on the logit of the score with a caliper.

Outcome regression that includes the same covariates is the other baseline tool. Doubly robust estimation, including augmented inverse-probability weighting, stays consistent if either the treatment model or the outcome model is right. It does not survive both models being wrong, and it does not repair a confounder that was never measured.

Treatment that continues, and confounders that react

Many clinical strategies are not a single start. A patient begins a drug, the response is observed, and the next visit changes the dose or stops the drug. The symptom that prompts the change is both a consequence of earlier treatment and a cause of later treatment and of the outcome. That is treatment-confounder feedback.

Putting every covariate, past and present, into one outcome regression blocks part of the treatment’s own path. The g-methods were built for this. Inverse-probability weighting of a marginal structural model reweights each visit by the probability of the treatment actually received given the past. The parametric g-formula fits the pieces of the data-generating process and then simulates the outcome under a strategy you impose, such as “always treat” against “never treat.” G-estimation of a structural nested model estimates the treatment effect at each visit without adjusting away the effect of earlier treatment. A 2024 Lancet Psychiatry paper on target trials in psychiatry states the split cleanly: baseline propensity methods are enough for two one-time strategies; g-methods are the ones to use when treatment and confounders keep updating each other.

The code tab includes a point-treatment g-formula and a two-period version. The two-period function is the smallest case with feedback: baseline state affects the first treatment, the first treatment affects the later state, and the later state affects the second treatment and the outcome. It is a synthetic check of the estimator, not a patient series.

An instrument in place of assignment

When important confounders are unmeasured, a variable that shifts treatment and has no other route to the outcome can stand in for randomization. An instrument has three properties: it predicts treatment, it shares no uncontrolled cause with the outcome, and it affects the outcome only through treatment. The exclusion restriction is the third. Two-stage least squares regresses treatment on the instrument, then regresses the outcome on that fitted treatment. The contrast is the effect among people whose treatment the instrument actually moves.

In electronic records the instrument is often a preference, a distance, a center’s policy, or a staffing shock that changes what is offered without changing the patient’s prognosis directly. Weak instruments move the estimate a long way for a little shift in treatment, and they do it with a wide interval. Mendelian randomization uses germline variants as the instrument for an exposure such as a biomarker or a drug target. Relevance, independence, and no horizontal pleiotropy are the same three properties in genetic language. Family designs, two-sample summary data, and methods for many weak variants are now standard around UK Biobank-scale studies. Pleiotropy is the usual way the exclusion restriction fails.

A policy with a start date

Interrupted time series is the design used when a guideline, a law, or a formulary change begins on a known day and the outcome is a rate over time. Segmented regression gives the series a level before the change, a slope before the change, a level shift at the change, and a slope shift after it. Bernal, Cummins, and Gasparrini’s tutorial is the usual reference. The impact model — a step, a slope change, or both — has to be chosen before looking at the result. Seasonality, autocorrelation, and a second event on the same day are the ordinary threats. A control series that was not subject to the policy is the check: a shift that appears in both series is a poor candidate for the policy effect.

Difference-in-differences compares the change in a group that received a policy with the change in a group that did not. It removes time-fixed differences under a parallel-trends assumption. It fits a formulary or a regional rule more often than it fits a drug chosen patient by patient, because individual treatment rarely creates two groups whose outcomes would have moved together.

A graph decides the adjustment set

A generic treatment question. Arrows are assumptions the analyst is willing to defend. They are not estimated from a dataset on this site.

Measured path Treatment to outcome Instrument Unmeasured back door

The BMJ guide for clinical researchers treats a directed acyclic graph as the way to name a minimally sufficient adjustment set: the smallest measured set that blocks every back-door path from treatment to outcome. Conditioning means regression, matching, stratification, or weighting. A mediator sits on the treatment’s own path; conditioning on it removes part of the effect you meant to keep. A collider is a common effect; conditioning on it opens a path that was closed. Selection into a study because of the treatment and because of the outcome is the usual collider in a chart review. An unmeasured confounder, drawn in gold, is the path a propensity score does not close. An instrument is useful only when the teal arrow is its only route to the outcome.

Checks that belong with the estimate

CheckWhat it is for
Covariate balanceAfter matching or weighting, measured confounders should look similar across strategies. A standardized difference that remains large is an open back door you claimed to close.
Positivity and weightsProbabilities near zero produce weights that are a handful of people. Trimming and overlap weights change the population. Say which population remains.
Negative controlsAn outcome the treatment cannot affect, or an exposure that cannot affect the real outcome, should show a null contrast. A signal there is residual bias.
Time zeroEligibility, assignment, and the start of follow-up are the same moment. Information from after that moment does not decide who enters.

The uveitis multi-database study is a useful picture of this discipline: an active-comparator new-user design, a posted protocol, a posted code path, propensity-score balance, and a result that is read across databases rather than from a single extract.
